# Proposal comparison and record guide

For each hotel retain a chronological submission and communication history and every proposal version. Each quote field should record value, currency, unit/basis, source reference (file/page or message), received date, and whether quoted, calculated, unknown, not applicable, or conflicting. Store dates and deadlines with time zone when known. Keep authorization history in the job record's `authorizations` array; link each external action to its covering authorization ID and retain superseded approvals. Keep an original source; never overwrite old terms. A correction should identify the old and new value and date.

Use these lifecycle states: `candidate`, `prepared`, `submitted`, `confirmed`, `awaiting_response`, `proposal_received`, `completed`; also record `declined` and outcome where applicable. Confirmation is evidence of receipt, not proof of a proposal. `awaiting_response` can follow either verified submission or confirmation. For uncertain sends, stay `prepared`, flag possible submission and check before retrying. Follow-up due is a flag/date, not proof of a response. A revised proposal remains `proposal_received` and adds a version. Close only on user direction.

## Workbook

Create a status sheet and a proposal comparison sheet, plus a detail sheet as needed. CSV fallback may use separate files. Track hotel/location, dates and availability, nightly rooms and rates, room nights, meeting rental, F&B, AV and required services, taxes, service charges, resort and other fees, concessions, commissionability and terms, deposits, cancellation, attrition, restrictions, source and update date. Record whether F&B is a minimum or a committed spend; do not add both if they overlap. Preserve original currency and per-night, per-room, per-person, percentage or fixed basis. Never sum different currencies without a separately documented exchange rate and date.

Calculate each known component from the documented basis and applicable quantity; apply tax and service charge only to documented bases, including order and compounding. Do not assume inclusions, taxability, mandatory charges or a zero for missing values. Avoid double counting packages, minima and inclusions. Known subtotal is the sum of substantiated components only; label it incomplete whenever any required cost or applicable charge is unknown. A complete estimated total requires every applicable component and charging basis to be known or explicitly not applicable. Keep blanks distinct from confirmed zero. Record exclusions and assumptions beside each comparison. List commissions separately; do not subtract them from client cost unless the user's agreement establishes a client credit. Do not assign invented values to concessions.

Summarize key differences, decision risks, missing values and reasons for any shortlist. Explain when differing dates, room nights, services or currencies prevent direct comparison. Never imply an incomplete subtotal is the final price.
